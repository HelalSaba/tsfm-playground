Evaluation seed produces small but systematic shifts in forecast and perturbation magnitudes, so numerical summaries are averaged over all five held-out evaluation seeds. In contrast, attention profiles and qualitative attention strategies are essentially unchanged within each trained checkpoint. Evaluation sampling therefore does not explain the observed differences in learned attention strategy.

Lag 2 is the modal top-attention lag in **5/8** independently trained AR(6) specialists. Lag 1 is not among the six highest-attended lags in **3/8** models and satisfies the broader attention-underrepresentation criterion in **3/8**. The exact strict original-style pattern occurs in **1/8** models. Therefore, the mismatch is reproducible but not universal: training seed changes the learned attention strategy, while perturbation continues to identify lag 1 as influential in the discrepant models.

Headline forecast metrics and perturbation-based support recovery are more stable across training seeds than exact attention rankings. Between 70% and 79% of positive perturbation importance remains inside the true first-p region, while top-p recall declines gradually from AR(2) to AR(7). Lag-1 underrepresentation also occurs in AR(7), indicating a seed-dependent high-order attention strategy rather than a uniquely AR(6) effect. Attention describes the broad recent-lag region but should not be treated as causal attribution of individual lags.
